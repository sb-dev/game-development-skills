P4 assesses material guidance against support, contrary approaches, alternatives and missing requirements. Completion means an evidence-qualified profile can be specified; it does not mean the methods have been empirically validated for a generated game.

## 1. Examined primary sources and independence

Only the identified source material supports the claims below. Text sections were examined; embedded games, videos and interactive demonstrations were not played. Developer descriptions establish a practice or declared intent, not its causal contribution to success. Documentation establishes described API/guideline behaviour, not a tested local installation.

| ID / source | Examined location and date / version | Evidence and limitation |
|---|---|---|

PPS-05–08 form one related guidance family, not four independent experiments. PPS-01 and PPS-02 are independent creators with different declared goals; neither is a controlled comparison. PPS-03 and PPS-04 address different layers of timing. The reviewed books remain the foundation for spatial and production reasoning, with their original dependencies and qualifications preserved. No primary human study was obtained that establishes an ideal platformer tolerance, difficulty or retry cost; those remain contextual choices requiring actual game/player evidence.

## 2. Claim dispositions and failure conditions

Standing and disposition are deliberately separate. The operational decisions in the final column are **this project's bounded adaptations**, not quotations or instructions attributed to the sources.

| Claim / P3 coverage | Supporting, contrary or alternative evidence | Evidential standing | Disposition and operational consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Response and boundary cases reveal different failures (PPF-01–02) | Swink's differentiated mapping and PPS-01's several distinct adjustments oppose one undifferentiated “responsive” setting | Context-dependent design method | **Retain / qualify.** Inspect mapping, input order, contact and cue separately. A passed average-response metric cannot dismiss missed boundary actions |
| Every precision platformer needs forgiveness (PPF-02) | PPS-01 describes choices for one game's particular verbs; P1 can legitimately have other accepted rules | Unsupported universal claim; documented practice in one game | **Reject the universal rule; retain an option.** Declare whether a tolerance exists and its semantics, expiry/consumption and reset behaviour. Changing it changes legal action opportunities and may affect routes; test both sides of the boundary and preserve approved rules |
| A maximum jump constant establishes level reachability (PPF-03, 05, 07, 11) | Totten's context-dependent metrics and Swink response analysis; PPS-03–04 expose timing assumptions behind recorded travel | Context-dependent method with technical prerequisites | **Adapt.** Use actual approach, input, contact and layout evidence under a declared runtime. A formula or privileged traversal is a useful bound, not a complete route or experience proof |
| One frame count or one fast-machine run establishes movement behaviour (PPF-01, 03, 06) | PPS-04 challenges render-frame assumptions; PPS-03 explains bounded-step/catch-up alternatives and their costs | Documented API behaviour plus engineering method | **Reject the sufficiency claim.** Include relevant elapsed-time, pause/resume and cadence conditions. Engineering chooses the implementation; the pack requires observable agreement with declared movement and safe recovery |
| A fixed timestep automatically guarantees correct physics and controls (PPF-01–03, 06) | PPS-03's headroom and interpolation trade-offs, plus P3's input/contact distinctions | Unsupported guarantee | **Reject.** Test the actual input path, collision and update ordering. A simulation trace and a browser-input run are different evidence; neither certifies all machines or network determinism |
| More cues/effects necessarily improve comprehension (PPF-04–05) | Swink/Totten already qualify excess effects; PPS-08 adds sensory-channel requirements | Practical heuristic with accessibility obligations | **Qualify.** Specify the hazard/landing information and when it is needed; check channel availability and obstruction. Human recognition still needs participants; visual similarity or cue count is insufficient |
| Shortest possible retry is always best (PPF-09–10) | PPS-02 deliberately presents substantial recovery cost; PPS-06 favours ways to reduce barriers | Conflicting production goals, not a settled universal | **Keep a soft diagnostic default only.** Focused repeats help investigate a movement fault; the shipped recovery policy follows declared intent/access needs. Test restart integrity independently of whether recovery is deliberately costly |
| Remapping plus a buffered jump establishes accessible precision play (PPF-04, 09) | PPS-05 identifies temporal, simultaneous and duration demands; PPS-06 offers per-mechanic alternatives | Specialist practical guidance; game-specific adequacy unresolved | **Reject sufficiency; extend coverage.** Record motor demands and chosen alternatives, then check the full action sequence. A hold toggle or slower mode needs its own movement/route checks and clear state; it must not silently alter the default profile |
| UI timing guidance defines a minimum jump window (PPF-02, 09–10) | PPS-07 explicitly excludes core gameplay mechanics | Supported scope distinction | **Reject the inference.** Review instruction/retry UI separately from action timing. An untimed restart control can coexist with demanding platforming |
| Focused attempts and a representative slice prove complete experience (PPF-08–10) | Fullerton controlled-test limits, Lemarchand representation limits, PPS-02's different recovery intent | Bounded production/evaluation method | **Retain the representations; reject overclaim.** Record omitted approach/session conditions. Use complete traversal and suitable human evidence for claims they cannot answer |
| A tiny edit is safe, or one isolated edit is always sufficient (PPF-05, 11) | Lemarchand's explicit jump-height dependency and PPF-03's coupling | Practical repair method, not a universal algorithm | **Qualify.** Identify affected accepted routes and compare plausible causes. Prefer the smallest adequate repair; allow a justified coordinated correction with the required authority and reruns |

## 3. Coverage extension and professional workflow

The original P2 map was revisited independently of the extracted findings. The most material additions are timing conditions, input-demand variants, UI-time scope and recovery-policy qualification. Broad core responsibilities remain core; the pack supplies specific traversal questions and affected-case selection.

| Responsibility / working artefact | Evidence-qualified practice and boundary | Remaining uncertainty / handling |
|---|---|---|
| Controller and level designers agree movement/space assumptions | Produce a compact movement record and route/landing cases together; PPF-01/03/06 plus PPS-03/04 explain why runtime conditions matter | Native physics/input implementation is engineering-owned; actual supported runtime must be measured later |
| Cheap representations resolve the current uncertainty | Diagrams can compare connections; a runnable sandbox resolves control/contact; an integrated approach resolves cue/timing coupling (PPF-08–10) | An automated input script is one actor under stated conditions, not a player population |
| Commitment to more rooms or finished content | Preserve accepted movement/clearance/cue agreements and demonstrate a representative retry loop before inferring repeatability (PPF-05/10/11) | No claim that one slice proves total cost or every future verb; record absent features |
| Input and difficulty options | Review the actual required simultaneous, held and timed action sequence, then choose task-specific alternatives (PPS-05/06) | Need participant evidence for intended access claims. Initial keyboard/browser qualification does not imply gamepad, touch or assistive-device support |
| Cue and retry interface handoff | Agree information, channels and player control of instructions/restart UI (PPS-07/08; PPF-04) | Audio, animation and art craft remain adjacent. Inspect actual integration and disclose sensory/task limitations |
| Diagnosis and correction | Reproduce approach/input/contact/outcome; distinguish controller, geometry, cue, test setup and runtime causes; rerun affected accepted routes | Human difficulty or frustration cannot be assigned a cause by deaths alone; preserve alternative explanations |
| Technical, automated and human review | Technical tests establish declared rules under tested conditions; observations/interviews answer recognition and experience questions (PPF-09, core evaluation contract) | No invented participants or global “feel score.” Expert repeat play is not first-use evidence |

The production relationship is sufficiently bounded to specify P5. Unresolved numeric preferences and human responses will be evaluated in the actual games, not silently promoted to fixed pack defaults. The absence of a universal ideal is not missing source access or a permission blocker. Networked control, specialised devices, moving-platform/dash/wall-jump rules and whole-game accessibility claims remain outside the initial research qualification unless later explicitly investigated and proved.
