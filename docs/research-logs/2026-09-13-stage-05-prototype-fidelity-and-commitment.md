## 2. Synthesis and decisions

This stage translates accepted research into a production decision contract. It does not repeat Stage 2 or claim new book/primary-source examination. No additional claims about current engines or services are needed for this strategy.

| Accepted basis | Decision made here | Limit retained |
|---|---|---|
| F3/L1: investigate the question with an adequate medium | Filter candidate representations for adequacy before comparing cost. | Paper, simulation and runnable proof answer different questions; none is universally cheapest. |
| L2 and Stage 2 R04: representative play and repeatable production are distinct claims | A slice names the dimensions it represents and the actual work supporting a commitment. | A polished corner, title or forecast cannot prove unrepresented breadth. |
| S1/S4 and Stage 2 R08: interaction depends on context | A sandbox includes the coupled input, camera, space or feedback required by its question. | “Isolated” does not mean stripping away the cause being investigated. |
| P08 and Stage 2 evidence classes | The proof record separates planned checks, actual results and unavailable conclusions. | Model success cannot establish comprehension or experience. |
| Stage 2 R14 and charter target-quality boundary | A performance question can require early representative target execution. | Fast editor iteration does not certify target behaviour. |
| L3/L4 and charter §6 | Include integration, review, rework and reversibility in cost; preserve valid authority and accepted dependencies. | No fixed schedule, universal disposal rule or extra approval cycle is inferred. |

The central decision is an **adequacy filter followed by a contextual cost comparison**. A weighted maturity or fidelity score is unnecessary: it could rank an attractive but invalid experiment above one that actually tests the question. A cost estimate remains an estimate until work is observed.

Fidelity is recorded across the relevant dimensions rather than collapsed into one level. This permits, for example, precise timing and functional sound with placeholder geometry, or a detailed resource simulation without a rendered scene. It also allows returning to cheap representations during late diagnosis.

### 2.1 Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Require all ten ladder steps in sequence | Contradicts the stage's explicit optional ladder and delays target-sensitive learning. |
| Choose the lowest creation effort regardless of validity | An omitted causal variable invalidates the proposed inference. |
| Produce final assets before judging a rule | Unnecessary unless the asset property itself carries the disputed behaviour/information. |
| Use “vertical slice” as sufficient acceptance evidence | The represented claim and actual output matter, not its label. |
| Treat eight candidate approvals as eight mandatory meetings | Adds bureaucracy and repeats authority already granted for bounded reversible work. |
| Automatically expand after a successful experiment | New scope may need different evidence, cost analysis and authority. |

## 3. Concrete selection and commitment review

These are **synthetic decision cases** used to inspect the written strategy. No new prototype, user study, network run, target profile, expenditure or owner approval is claimed. The prior Stage 4 arithmetic is referenced only for its recorded model result.

| Case / known question | Options and cost/adequacy comparison | Decision under the contract | Verification finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: Does the free-upgrade formula violate the positive-cost rule? | Written reasoning and the existing bounded arithmetic preserve the quantities; a rendered shop adds input/art work unrelated to that invariant. | Use rule/model evidence for the invariant. Keep pacing and human-balance questions open. | Correctly selects cheap adequate evidence without a playable scene; Stage 4's result is not re-labelled as game balance. |
| B: Does crossing a gap remain controllable with the intended moving camera? | Paper omits temporal input/camera coupling; an avatar-only sandbox omits the camera; a full level adds unrelated content. | Choose a controller/camera sandbox with relevant collision and spacing, placeholder visuals and scoped human/measurement questions. | Selects the minimum coupled scenario, not the nominally smallest artefact. |
| C: Can a new player recognise an audio warning and its alternative cue? | Silent placeholder play removes the disputed information; final environment art adds unrelated work; a small scene with functional cues preserves the question. | Use the small scene and relevant human observation. If required participants are unavailable, the comprehension claim stays blocked. | Minimal content still includes essential feedback and access conditions; simulation cannot substitute for the human claim. |
| D: Does reconnect during a reward event duplicate the outcome? | A single local run cannot expose the distributed event; a complete online world adds unrelated services/content. | Select a minimal multi-peer/session proof with declared adverse conditions and the reward rule. Resolve implementation prerequisites later for actual execution. | Preserves network authority/timing rather than treating local gameplay as proof. No network run is claimed. |
| E: Does a candidate meet a target workload budget? | An editor sample is convenient but not representative; a target stress scene preserves the affected workload without all final content. | Use a representative target build/scene and stated measurement boundaries. Missing target access blocks the target claim. | Does not postpone necessary target evidence to a fixed ladder position. |
| F: A producer proposes many new encounters based on one visually finished room | That room demonstrates only its observed integration/content conditions. Unseen encounter types and repetition/review costs remain unknown. | Present a bounded scope proposal and the missing production questions; obtain any new material commitment from its owner after reviewable evidence. | Does not equate finish with evidence for volume or manufacture approval. |
| G: A proposed timing adjustment stays inside an already accepted range | The affected trace and cases can be rechecked cheaply; no new spend, scope or accepted constraint changes. | Proceed within existing authority and record the result/impact. | Does not add an approval prompt merely because a listed commitment category exists. |
| H: The same adjustment exceeds the accepted range and breaks a required route | Small edit size hides an accepted dependency and broader re-evaluation cost. | Prepare the specific reopen proposal, affected route evidence and alternatives; wait for the designated decision before changing it. | Distinguishes repair scope from edit size and preserves accepted work. |

The contract was also read backwards from each proposed output: could its evidence answer the named question, and what claim would remain unsupported? Each case identifies that boundary. Synthetic missing participants or target access test the rule's response; they are not missing prerequisites for this document-design stage.
